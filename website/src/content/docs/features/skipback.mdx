<Badge text="c1.4 Feature" variant="tip" />

Skipback keeps the last few seconds of what you played in memory, all the time, so a good moment can be saved after
it happened, without having pressed record. Launch a few clips, jam on the keyboard or a guitar, and when something
good comes out, go back and keep it as a sample, a clip and the notes you played.

Nothing touches the SD card until you save.

## Setting it up

The settings are at the end of `SETTINGS > COMMUNITY FEATURES`:

- `Skipback (SKBK)`: how many seconds to keep: `OFF` (default), `10`, `20`, `30` or `60` seconds. While it's `OFF`,
  nothing is kept and no memory is used.
- `Skipback Source (SKSR)`: whose audio to keep:
  - `Selected clip (CLIP)` (default): the track of the clip that is selected, after its own FX and as loud as it is
    heard in the mix, but without the song's FX and without its reverb send.
  - `Master (MAST)`: the whole output.
- `Skipback Record (SKRC)`: when to keep:
  - `Always (ALWA)` (default): all the time.
  - `While playing (PLAY)`: only while the transport plays. Each `PLAY` starts over, and what a play kept stays after
    you stop, until the next `PLAY` - so you can stop and tweak sounds without losing the take.

## Playing

- **Synths, kits, sample tracks:** select the clip of the track you want to keep (open it, or tap it in song view)
  and play. That track is kept.
- **A guitar or anything else on the line input:** just plug it in. While something is plugged into the line input
  (or the mic input), it is kept as well, as it comes in, dry - whatever clip is selected and whether you hear it
  through the Deluge or not. No audio track is needed.
- **Notes** played live (keyboard, audition pads, MIDI in) on synths, MIDI and CV instruments are kept too.

## Saving what you just played

1. **Hold `RECORD` and press `SELECT`.** The skipback screen opens and stops keeping, so nothing more is lost.
   - The pads show a waveform of what was kept, oldest on the left.
   - The last phrase you played is already chosen - in whole bars if the transport was playing - between a green
     start column and a red end column.
   - The OLED shows whose audio it is (the track's name, and `+ LINE IN` if the line input has sound), how long the
     range is, and how many notes are in it.
2. **Adjust the range if needed:**
   - **A main pad** moves the nearer marker (start or end) to that column.
   - **Turning `SELECT`** nudges the marker last moved: by a 1/16 note if the transport was playing, otherwise by
     10 ms, or 1 ms with `SHIFT`.
   - **The horizontal encoder** scrolls; with `SHIFT`, or pressed, it zooms.
3. **Listen:** an audition pad (right column) plays the range in place of the output; press it again to stop.
4. **Save:** press `SELECT` (or `RECORD` + `SELECT` again). `BACK` leaves without saving.

## What saving makes

- Each source with sound in the range - the selected clip's track (or the master), and the line input - goes to a
  WAV file of its own in `SAMPLES/SKIPBACK/` and into a muted audio clip of its own on a new track.
- The notes played in the range go into a muted clip next to each instrument's clip, in a section of its own.
- All of them are as long as the range, so they line up when launched. Launch them like any other clip.

The popup says what was saved, for example `Saved REC00000 + REC00001 + 1 note clip`.

<Aside type="caution">
  Selecting a clip of another track starts the keeping of the track's audio over, for that track. The line input's
  audio carries on.
</Aside>

## Memory

The audio is kept as 16-bit stereo in the sample cache's memory: 176 KB a second, so 5.3 MB for 30 seconds. With
something plugged into the line input, that's kept as well, in a second ring that costs as much again. The line
input's ring is set up when something is first plugged in and stays until the setting changes.

## Limits

- The selected track is kept without the song's FX (master filters, compressor) and without its reverb send, which
  goes to the song's shared reverb.
- Notes played on kits aren't kept as notes (their sound is).
- If a guitar also goes through an audio track that is selected and monitored, it is kept twice: through the
  track's FX, and dry from the line input.
